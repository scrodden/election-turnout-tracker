Florida reports who voted by party but not how each party voted. If every Republican who cast a ballot voted for the Republican and every Democrat for the Democrat, whatever is left of each county's result is how NPAs and minor-party voters voted. That leftover is the implied NPA margin.

How this is figured, and where it breaks

Implied NPA margin = (Republican's votes − Democrat's votes − (Republican ballots − Democratic ballots)) ÷ (NPA + minor-party ballots). Positive is a Republican lead.

In other words, the result's margin in votes minus the margin Republicans had over Democrats in ballots cast, spread over the voters who are neither. Minor-party voters (about 3% of ballots) are counted with NPAs, so the figure assumes they voted like NPAs. Everyone in the pool who voted for a third candidate or skipped the race falls under “other/blank”.

Where it breaks

  • Crossover voting. Some Democrats vote Republican and some Republicans vote Democratic, and the method assigns all of that to NPAs. Hatched counties are where a candidate got fewer votes than ballots cast by their own party's voters, so the assumption plainly fails there. This happens mostly in rural North Florida, where many longtime registered Democrats vote Republican. There, the implied margins run past 100 points.
  • Turnout counts vs. ballots counted. Party turnout is the counties' check-in and ballot-return counts by party; results are ballots tabulated. Where they disagree, the gap lands on NPAs. The table's last column (votes/ballots) compares votes in the race with ballots cast; dashed counties are where votes exceed ballots with a party on file by more than 0.5% (party turnout incomplete), or fall below 95% of them.
  • Lake County reports Election Day party only on its website's hourly chart, and the last one posted is for 6 pm, while polls closed at 7 pm. Its final hour of Election Day ballots has no party, so its implied NPA figure picks up however that hour's voters voted.
  • By vote method, the same arithmetic runs on one way of voting at a time: a county's mail (or early, or Election Day) votes for each candidate against that method's ballots by party. Only about two-thirds of the state's ballots can be split this way. Florida's statewide results carry no vote types, so these come from each county's own results site, and Broward, Pinellas, Volusia, Osceola, Martin and more than a dozen VR Systems counties (Brevard, Manatee, Marion, Escambia and others) publish totals only. Mail “ballots” are mail ballots returned, and a few returned ballots are rejected, so mail votes run slightly under them.
  • Results are unofficial election-night returns until counties certify, and provisional ballots aren't in the party turnout.
