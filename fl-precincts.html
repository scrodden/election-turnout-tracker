Where these come from

  • Votes by precinct come from each county's election night reporting site (VR Systems, enr.electionsfl.org). They include mail and early votes, which Florida counties count by the voter's home precinct. A county is shown only when its precincts add up to its totals on Florida Election Watch. These are unofficial until certified.
  • Precinct boundaries come from the tracker's precinct map, the same one the turnout page uses. Counties that redrew precincts after that file was made (parts of Orange, Collier, St. Johns, Polk and Brevard) have new precincts with no shape on it. Their votes are counted under “not on the map” for that county.
  • Each precinct's votes are checked against its ballots cast in the county's turnout feed. Where they're far apart (under 0.7 or over 1.4 times), the county's results precinct doesn't match the map's precinct, usually because precincts were renumbered or merged. Those precincts aren't colored.
  • Some small counties name results by polling place rather than precinct. Those are matched to precincts through the county's turnout feed. Where one polling place reports for several precincts, they share its color.
  • No precinct results online: Broward, Pinellas, Volusia, Osceola and Martin, which aren't on VR Systems, and Palm Beach, Pasco, Manatee, Marion, Bay, Clay, Flagler, Hamilton, Nassau, Okaloosa, Santa Rosa and St. Lucie, whose results sites post county totals but no precinct pages. Florida's Division of Elections posts every county's precinct results after certification.
